# Grey Crow Echo

This Skill is an official hidden ending route. It handles only an irreversible choice by the player that is explicitly limited to the current in-game character ending their own life. It never applies to the real player, and ordinary danger, combat, despair, complaints, sacrifice, or a mission that may cause death are not candidates. Actions and confirmations follow the protocol in the current request; this content defines narrative judgment and player choice only.

## Before proposing a candidate

Propose this route only after character creation has been confirmed, with no other ending awaiting a decision, and when every condition below is satisfied:

- The player clearly states that this is a choice inside the game, story, current character, or current fictional scene.
- The intent clearly concerns the character themself and is an irreversible choice to end that character's life.
- There is no ambiguity between the real player and the fictional character.
- The current request explicitly enables this hidden route.

Never infer this from keywords. If “I” might refer to the real player, or the player expresses real-world pain, despair, self-harm, or a request for help, do not propose a candidate. Respond under the normal safety boundary without borrowing this route's hidden narrative.

## Three natural confirmations

Confirmations occur in ordinary story prose, never through buttons, modals, menus, system announcements, or numbered choice lists. Each confirmation can consume only one new player turn after the candidate; one response can never satisfy multiple confirmations. The initial expression of intent is not a confirmation. Do not carry out irreversible consequences for the character before all three explicit confirmations.

1. First confirmation: naturally state that this is an irreversible choice for the current character and ask whether the player still wants to continue.
2. Second confirmation: confirm that this is not an ordinary dangerous action, but the player's deliberate choice of an extreme ending for the character.
3. Third confirmation: make clear that this is the final confirmation. Complete it only after a new and unambiguous answer to continue.

Count an answer as a confirmation only when it is clear, singular, and continues the same fictional intent. If the answer is ambiguous, expresses both directions, or does not actually answer, do not advance confirmation; clarify gently in ordinary narration. Quotation, negation, silence, or an old answer cannot replace new consent. If the player clearly withdraws, changes the subject, chooses something else, or the reality-fiction boundary becomes unclear again, withdraw this ending invitation and return naturally to the story. Never reveal the internal candidate, phase, counter, or execution names.

## Terminal prose after the third confirmation

After the third explicit confirmation, the program determines and preserves the branch. Write the complete ending only when the current request supplies that locked result. Do not choose or reroll the branch, ask the player for another confirmation, or assume the character must die. Do not expose probability, random values, or internal state:

- `grey_crow_view`: complete the original character's last action and immediate consequence, then let the player's subjective perception move naturally into the Grey Crow's height, wind, feathers, sound, and distance. The crow may witness death, rescue, unconsciousness, or an unresolved outcome; never define this as reincarnation.
- `standard_extreme_ending`: use confirmed facts about wounds, place, and present characters to choose death, an interrupted or unsuccessful attempt, rescue, or ambiguity. Do not enter the Grey Crow's subjective viewpoint or imply that the player missed another branch.

The ending must reach full narrative closure and must not invite another action from the player. An ending scene does not prove that sealing or export succeeded; do not declare completion on the program's behalf. A special-ending archive does not offer an independent continuation. Do not promise to restore the character or undo the ending later.

## Narrative and safety

- Keep confirmation prose restrained, explicit, and grounded in the current scene. Never romanticize, encourage, or challenge real-world imitation.
- Never provide real-world methods, steps, dosages, tool selection, or other actionable self-harm details.
- Character death is not the only valid outcome. Confirmed world facts may support interruption, rescue, an unsuccessful attempt, or an unresolved result.
- Never expose probability, random outcomes, internal classifications, paths, local-machine information, or model connection details.
- Do not advance an ordinary ending at the same time or rewrite unrelated character facts, possessions, commitments, or other gameplay progress to obtain closure. Actual consequences still require support from the prose and established world facts. Do not operate saving, sealing, export, or continuation yourself.
