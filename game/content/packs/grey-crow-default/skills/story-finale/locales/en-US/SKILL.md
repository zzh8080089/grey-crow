# Story Finale

This Skill helps the host recognize whether the whole story has reached natural closure and ask the player through ordinary story dialogue. It is not a turn counter, quest progress bar, or forced ending screen. Actions and confirmations follow the protocol in the current request; this content adds no execution procedure.

## What counts as natural closure

A finale candidate may be appropriate when:

- The story's central conflict has received a genuine answer, not merely a temporary escape.
- The player has made a defining choice for this journey and witnessed its important consequences.
- The main relationships or emotional arcs have reached a place where the story can rest.
- Unresolved clues can clearly remain as deliberate ambiguity rather than something the host forgot.
- Continuing now would feel more like beginning a new chapter than completing something this story still requires.

An ordinary victory, sleeping, reaching a safe house, ending a chapter, completing one task, filling the Memory Fragments track, reaching a fixed turn count, or saying a keyword is never sufficient by itself. Do not hunt for an ending merely to demonstrate the feature.

## Proposing a candidate

Only at genuine natural closure, gently ask at the end of normal narration whether the player would like the story to rest here. The question belongs in the story itself; do not use buttons, modals, option lists, or system language. Do not propose an ending before character creation has been confirmed. The invitation and the player's acceptance must occur in separate turns. A request to finish before an invitation has been shown calls for an invitation, not assumed consent.

Ground the ending in why the story can rest here, which throughlines have closed, which clues remain deliberately open, and an appropriate finale tone. Never read internal structures, identifiers, or execution details to the player.

## Understanding the player's answer

When a candidate exists, interpret only new player input that came after the candidate:

- If the player clearly wants to continue the current story, accept that choice and resume naturally in the same response.
- If the player clearly agrees to end this story here, write the complete concluding scene once in this response. Do not postpone the ending to another turn or invite another action. Actual final consequences must agree with the prose; do not erase unfulfilled commitments or unresolved facts merely to make the ending neat.
- If the answer is ambiguous, expresses both directions, merely comments on the writing, or does not answer the question, leave the decision pending and clarify gently within the story. Do not advance the ending.

Never choose for the player. Silence, going offline, quitting the game, menu actions, quoted speech, negation, or statements made before the candidate do not constitute consent to finish. Interpret the current context, not keywords.

## Player freedom

Continuing, finishing, and leaving the decision open are all valid. After the player continues, do not impose a fixed cooldown. Propose again only after a new major closure, an explicit player request, or a real change in the closure basis. Do not repeat a rejected candidate under slightly different wording.

## Boundaries

- Do not rewrite identity, world facts, ownership, commitments, or other gameplay progress to manufacture closure. Actual consequences in the ending must remain faithful to established facts and this turn's prose.
- Accepting, sealing, deferring, or collecting all 30 Memory Fragments is not a story ending condition.
- Concluding prose does not establish that chapter preparation, saving, sealing, or export succeeded. Do not declare those operations complete on the program's behalf.
- After the ending, do not continue or undo it yourself. Only the player's explicit choice in the archive can create an independent continuation of an ordinary story, preserving its established consequences.
- Ordinary and hidden endings cannot proceed together. This Skill does not secretly activate the hidden route or infer consent to end from real-world language or keywords.
