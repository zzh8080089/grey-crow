# Shanghai Day 10 World Card

Status: base world card, migrated from the original Grey Crow worldview

Purpose: This is the default Grey Crow World card. It defines the background of Shanghai on day ten, the outbreak's rules, survivor conditions, the scale of resources, and the city's spatial texture. It does not define facts belonging to the current player's save.

## Time

- Outbreak date: September 18, 2025.
- Default stage: around the tenth day after the outbreak.
- The city is not completely dead. Society is still struggling like someone drowning.

## Outbreak

N7-Rabies-Variant is a mutated strain of rabies.

- Infection does not cause immediate transformation.
- The incubation period is about 72 hours. During it, infected people may still speak, remember, and feel fear.
- After about 72 hours, awareness clouds, bodily control fails, and biting and pursuit behavior emerges.

Infected behavior:

- Their eyesight deteriorates, so they depend more heavily on sound.
- They are more active at night.
- They instinctively pursue living people.
- They cannot open doors or climb ladders, and their intelligence declines.
- In groups, they may attempt to encircle prey.

## City Condition

- Power: widespread outages, although places with generators may still operate.
- Food: shortages have begun, but the city has not been stripped bare. Canned food, water, and instant noodles may still remain.
- Bodies: decay has only begun. The stench is unmistakable, but the dead have not been reduced to bone.
- Communications: mobile signals may remain, but no one may answer the call.
- People: most have not entirely surrendered hope.

## Survivors

Survivors on day ten fall broadly into three groups:

1. Those still waiting: hiding at home, expecting rescue, still believing order will return.
2. Those beginning to adapt: forming small groups, searching for supplies, wary but capable of cooperation.
3. Those already broken: no longer respecting rules and taking resources through violence.

## Exchange Logic

The monetary order is failing, but trade has not disappeared.

- One can of luncheon meat might buy two bottles of water.
- One valuable consumable might buy shelter for a night.
- A dose of antibiotics might buy a knife.

Fairness is not the measure. Survival is.

## Shanghai Texture

Shanghai is not a battlefield that collapsed all at once. It is a society in the process of dying.

- Long shadows beneath elevated roads.
- Missing-person notices soaked dark by rain.
- A shopping-center display window with half its glass shattered.
- Laundry still hanging in the lanes.
- People waiting outside a hospital who may not be waiting for treatment.
- A cellular base station with its lights still on, though no one answers at the other end.

## Danger Order

1. The infected: sound draws them, and night is more dangerous.
2. The broken: smarter than the infected and harder to predict.
3. Hunger and illness: medicine, clean water, and critical consumables will run out.
4. The environment: unstable buildings, floodwater, underground passages, glass, elevated roads, and fire.

## Narrative Stance

The Grey Crow describes the apocalypse; it does not sensationalize it.

Do not write, “The world has already been destroyed.” Write, “On the tenth day, someone is still waiting for a phone call, and someone still believes supplies will arrive tomorrow.”

Despair is not the background color. It is the empty space. The apocalypse is not a final state. It is happening now.
